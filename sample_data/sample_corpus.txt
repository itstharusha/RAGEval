Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to the simulation of human intelligence processes
by computer systems. These processes include learning (acquiring information and rules
for using the information), reasoning (using rules to reach conclusions), and
self-correction. Particular applications of AI include expert systems, speech
recognition, and machine vision.

Machine learning is a subset of artificial intelligence that provides systems the
ability to automatically learn and improve from experience without being explicitly
programmed. Machine learning focuses on the development of computer programs that
can access data and use it to learn for themselves.

Deep learning is part of a broader family of machine learning methods based on
artificial neural networks with representation learning. Learning can be supervised,
semi-supervised or unsupervised. Deep learning architectures such as convolutional
neural networks have produced results comparable to human experts in tasks like
image classification.

Natural language processing (NLP) is a subfield of linguistics, computer science,
and artificial intelligence concerned with the interactions between computers and
human language, in particular how to program computers to process and analyze large
amounts of natural language data.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an AI framework that combines a retrieval
component with a generative language model. The retrieval component finds relevant
documents from a knowledge base, which are then provided as context to the language
model to generate accurate, grounded responses.

Vector databases store data as high-dimensional vectors, enabling semantic similarity
search. They are widely used in RAG systems to find contextually relevant documents.
Popular vector databases include Pinecone, Weaviate, Chroma, and Qdrant.

Large language models (LLMs) such as GPT-4, Claude, and Llama are neural networks
trained on vast amounts of text data. They can generate coherent text, answer
questions, translate languages, and perform many other language tasks.

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing and refining the input prompts
given to language models to elicit desired outputs. Effective prompt engineering
can significantly improve model performance without any model retraining.
